[Atlas Agent](/docs/agents/atlas-agent) runs through the Atlas gateway, and every request it makes is billed to an organization. So it needs two things: a synced [organization](/docs/team/organizations) to bill, and AI access for that organization.

Other agents don't. Claude Code, Codex and any other ACP agent run on your own subscription or keys, and work the same in a local organization.

## When the composer says Atlas Agent can't run

If Atlas Agent can't run, a bar above its composer says why, and the composer stays disabled until it's resolved.

**The organization is local.** The bar names your organization and says it is local. The gateway can't bill an organization that only exists on your machine. Select **Turn on sync** on the bar, or switch to a synced organization. Signed out, the button starts sign-in first.

**The organization doesn't have AI grants.** The organization is synced but has no AI access yet. There is no setting you can change to fix this; access has to be granted. Select **Request** to open credits.tryatlas.cc and ask Atlas for it, then **Refresh** to check again once it's granted.

Atlas only shows the second bar when the gateway explicitly says there is no grant. If it simply can't reach the gateway, for example offline, it doesn't tell you to ask anyone for access.

## Who grants AI access

Atlas grants AI access to an organization as a plan: a monthly budget, optionally a daily one, and the models it covers. Access is off until a plan exists. **Request** on the composer bar opens credits.tryatlas.cc, where you ask Atlas for one.

Within the plan, an organization's admins decide how it is used, in the web app under **AI**:

- **Default model**: where a new session starts when it doesn't name one. **Atlas's recommendation** follows Atlas's pick as it changes; or choose a specific model.
- **Models**: turn individual models off for the organization. A model that's off is refused by the gateway, not just hidden. Admins can narrow what the plan allows, never widen it.

Everyone in the organization can read this page. Only admins can change it.

In the desktop app, Atlas Agent's model picker offers only the models your organization's access allows.

## Budgets and per-person caps

The organization's budget is shown under **Limits** on the **AI** page, as **Monthly budget** and **Daily budget**. Both read **Set by your plan**: nobody in the organization can change them in the web app, admins included. When the organization reaches a cap, Atlas stops accepting new requests until it resets.

A plan can also carry a cap for one member: a ceiling on that person's spend inside the organization's budget. It is a second limit, not a second wallet. Their spend still counts against the organization. Only Atlas staff can set a per-person cap, so an admin who wants one asks Atlas. A member with a cap sees it on their **Usage** page as **Your monthly cap** or **Your daily cap**.

## See who spent what

The web app's **Usage** page shows what the organization has spent over **1d**, **7d** or **30d**. It keeps two figures apart:

- **Measured**: spend through the Atlas gateway, billed by Atlas. This is Atlas Agent and Atlas's own AI features.
- **Estimated**: your recorded sessions priced at list rates. These are agents running on people's own subscriptions and keys, which Atlas never bills.

Beside them are **Sessions** and **Daily spend**. Daily spend is a single number, the average per day over the range, with a small sparkline of the days in the range. There is no daily chart. Below the figures are the budget and a **By member** table with each person's measured spend, estimated spend and session count.

Admins see the whole organization and every member's share. Everyone else sees only their own figures. Per-person spend is there so an admin can decide whether to ask Atlas for a cap on someone.

The desktop app's [Usage](/docs/context/usage) tab is different: it reports what was recorded on your machine, across every agent, over **7d**, **30d**, **90d**, **All** or a custom range.

## Your prompts

Atlas's billing records count tokens and cost. They don't store what you asked.

For troubleshooting, an organization admin can open a **prompt capture** window in the web app under **Privacy**. For a few hours, up to a day, the gateway keeps the prompts and replies of the organization's requests so Atlas support can investigate a problem. Each window is turned on separately for the **Coding surface** (what your desktop agent sends) and **Atlas features**. It stops by itself, never applies to past requests, and the captured prompts are deleted after a retention period. Nobody at Atlas can turn capture on for you.
