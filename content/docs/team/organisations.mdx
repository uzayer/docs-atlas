An [organisation](/docs/getting-started/concepts#organisation) owns your projects. Use the switcher at the top of the sidebar to create, select, rename, or delete one.

## Choose local or cloud

Create a **local** organisation when you work on one machine. It stays on that machine and never makes a network call.

Create a **cloud** organisation when you need an account-backed organisation with members and roles. It appears on your other signed-in devices. Cloud organisations currently sync the organisation record, not your code, sessions, chats, memory, or timeline.

You can turn on sync for a local organisation later. Atlas keeps its name and handle, then links it to your account.

## Create and switch organisations

Select **Create organisation…**, choose a name, handle, and type, then create it. Cloud handles must be unique across Atlas; local handles need only be unique on your machine.

Atlas currently hosts cloud organisations in the United States. European Union hosting appears in the picker but is not available yet.

Select an organisation from the switcher to move into it. Atlas remembers the project you last opened in each organisation.

Cloud organisations stay locked when you are signed out or not a member. Local organisations never lock.

## Rename or sync one

Hover a local organisation and select the pencil to rename it. Renaming changes the displayed name, not its handle.

A synced organisation's name is owned by the server, so Atlas does not offer a local rename action for it. Names changed on the web are pulled into the desktop app when the account refreshes.

For an active local organisation, choose **Turn on sync** to create its cloud record. Atlas does not offer a sync-off switch. Delete the organisation to remove it from your account.

## Delete an organisation

Hover an organisation and select the bin. Atlas removes its project records and chat history, but never touches the actual project files on disk.

You cannot delete your last organisation. Deleting a cloud organisation requires Admin access; without it, Atlas removes only the local copy.
