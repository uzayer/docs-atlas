The handbook is where your team writes down how it works: conventions, decisions, processes and how the system fits together. People read it, agents read it, and nobody changes it without a reviewed proposal.

## Why a handbook

Atlas already keeps two kinds of context, and neither is a team's agreed documentation:

- [Memory](/docs/context/memory) is what agents noticed. Agents write it freely, and it lives per repository on each person's machine.
- The [knowledge base](/docs/context/knowledge-base) is notes you write deliberately, but they sit in `.atlas/knowledge/`, which is gitignored, so they stay on one machine.

The handbook is the third kind: what the team agreed, versioned in git and shared by everyone.

## How it will work

**Pages live in git.** Handbook pages are Markdown files in a repository, with an owner for each page. You read and edit them in Atlas, and they version like code.

**Every change is a proposal.** Whether a person or an agent writes it, a change arrives for review with:

- the diff
- why it is being made
- the page's owner, who reviews it
- the other pages it affects, such as pages that link to it
- where it came from: the chat thread, call, comment or agent session where the decision was made

Agents can draft and propose changes. Only people merge them.

**Propose from where the decision happened.** When a chat thread, a call or a comment thread reaches a decision, you will be able to hand it to an agent, which finds the right page, fits the change in and opens the proposal.

**Catch pages that drift.** A page can name the code it describes. When that code changes and the page does not, Atlas flags the page, and can draft the update as a proposal for its owner.

**Agents cite what they read.** Agents will search the handbook and link the exact page and version they relied on, so you can check an answer against its source. Your `AGENTS.md` can point at the handbook rather than copy it.

## Where the idea comes from

We are borrowing from GitLab's handbook-first practice. The handbook describes what the team does now, not what it hopes to do, and you change a process by changing the handbook. Git splits proposing from approving, so anyone can propose and the owner only has to review.

Writing those changes well takes effort, which is the part agents can take on. Keeping pages fresh is the part most handbooks never automate, which is why drift detection is part of the plan.

## Until then

- Keep team conventions in your repository's `CLAUDE.md` or `AGENTS.md`, and let Atlas [mirror one into the other](/docs/context/memory#mirror-your-instructions-into-agentsmd).
- Ship repeatable procedures as [skills](/docs/context/skills).
- Use the [knowledge base](/docs/context/knowledge-base) for your own notes, and [shared memory](/docs/context/memory) for what agents learn.

Follow progress on the [roadmap](/docs/community/roadmap).
