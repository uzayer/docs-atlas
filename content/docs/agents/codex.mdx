Codex is the other agent Atlas supports in depth. If you already use the Codex CLI, there's almost nothing to set up. Atlas picks up the credentials you've got.

## Install

Nothing to install by hand. Atlas launches Codex through `npx`, which fetches the ACP adapter on first run, so the only prerequisite is Node.js. If it's missing, Atlas tells you exactly that and points you at nodejs.org.

<Callout type="info">
  Atlas reads your login shell's `PATH` at startup, so a `node` installed via nvm, fnm, volta, asdf, or Homebrew is found even when you launch Atlas from the Dock rather than a terminal.
</Callout>

## Sign in

**You probably already are.** Codex inherits authentication from your environment and your Codex config directory: the ChatGPT login you did through the Codex CLI, or an `OPENAI_API_KEY` in your environment.

Atlas doesn't ask you to log in again and doesn't store a separate credential of its own.

If Codex fails to start with an authentication error, fix it the way you would for the CLI: sign in with Codex directly, then come back.

## Models

Codex exposes its model as a configuration option over ACP, and Atlas switches it through the composer's model picker. Your selection applies to the session you're in.

## Modes

Codex advertises its modes over ACP, and they appear in the composer's mode picker alongside the model.

## Sessions

Atlas records the session itself: it lands in your [timeline](/docs/getting-started/concepts#timeline), and Atlas links commits made during it to that session as [checkpoints](/docs/getting-started/concepts#checkpoint), so the history you care about is Atlas's, not the agent's.

What Codex doesn't have is the on-disk transcript parsing [Claude Code](/docs/agents/claude-code#sessions) gets, where Atlas reads the agent's own log files to rebuild a conversation. That's specific to Claude Code's format.
