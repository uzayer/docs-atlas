An [organisation](/docs/getting-started/concepts#organisation) is the top-level container that owns your [projects](/docs/getting-started/concepts#project). Exactly one is active per window.

The switcher sits at the top of the sidebar, showing the active organisation's name. Click it for the full list, the create action, member management, and the sync state.

## Cloud and local

When you create an organisation you pick one of two types, and the difference is where it exists.

| | Cloud | Local |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requires an account | Yes | No |
| Handle checked globally | Yes | Only against your other organisations |
| Members and roles | Yes | No |
| Available on your other devices | Yes | No |
| Region | United States | Not hosted anywhere |

A local organisation is a real choice, not a downgrade. It never makes a network call, and if you're working alone that's usually the right one.

You can link it to your account later with **Turn on sync**, which is the same path a signed-out user's organisations take once they sign in.

Signed out, cloud is unavailable rather than merely unselected (there's no account to create it in), and the type is pinned to local.

### What "synced" means

The switcher describes a cloud organisation as synced, and offers **Turn on sync** for a local one. It's worth being exact about the scope of that word.

Syncing an organisation means **the organisation itself lives in your Atlas account**: its name, its handle, its members, and their roles. That record is available on any device you sign in on, which is why the organisation appears in the switcher on a machine you've never used it on before.

It does not mean your work syncs. Your [sessions](/docs/getting-started/concepts#session), chats, [memory](/docs/context/memory), and [timeline](/docs/source-control/timeline) stay on the device that produced them.

Your code is shared the way it already was, through git.

  More will sync over time. Today the account record is the whole of it, and the roadmap is where that story continues.
</Callout>

## Creating one

**Create organisation…** in the switcher opens a form with four fields.

**Name** is a display name and can be anything, though it has to be unique among your own organisations.

**Handle** is the URL-safe identifier, shown under the `app.tryatlas.cc/` prefix. It's derived from the name as you type and stops tracking it the moment you edit the handle directly.

Lowercase letters, numbers, and hyphens only.

For a cloud organisation the handle is checked against every Atlas organisation as you type, and the field reports **That handle is available** or **That handle is already taken**. The check is advisory (the server holds the real guard), so a create can still fail on a handle claimed a moment before yours.

That comes back as an error, and nothing half-made is left behind: the server settles the handle before anything is written locally.

A local organisation's handle isn't probed, because it lives only on your machine and there's no shared namespace to check it against.

**Type** is cloud or local, covered above.

**Region** applies only to a cloud organisation. United States is available; European Union is shown but not selectable, because it isn't hosted yet.

Creating an organisation switches you into it.

## Switching

Pick any organisation in the list to switch. Atlas tears down the current one and reloads, showing a loading overlay while it does.

Each organisation remembers the project you had open, so switching back lands you where you were.

### Access and locked organisations

A cloud organisation belongs to an account, so Atlas won't leave you inside one that isn't yours. A locked row shows a padlock, can't be selected, and offers no rename or delete.

- **Signed out**: every cloud organisation is locked. *Sign in to open this synced organisation.*
- **Signed in, and you're not a member**: locked. *This account isn't a member of this organisation.*
- **Signed in, but your membership isn't known yet**: allowed. On a machine that has never successfully listed your organisations, Atlas can't prove you don't belong, and locking you out of your own organisation offline is the worse mistake.

Local organisations are never locked. They need no credential.

The refresh button in the switcher header pulls your organisation list from the server again. It's quiet on failure: the last known list stays.

## Renaming

Hover any unlocked row and click the pencil for an inline rename. Names are unique among your organisations, case-insensitively, so a collision is refused with a message rather than silently applied.

Renaming is local. It changes what you see in the switcher, not the handle, and not the name on the server.

## Turning on sync

For the active local organisation, the switcher footer offers **Turn on sync for *name*…**. It creates the organisation in your Atlas account under its existing name and handle, and links the two.

Signed out, this starts sign-in instead, and signing in re-pulls your organisation list anyway.

Once linked, the footer reads *name* **is synced** and the action is gone. Turning sync off isn't offered: to stop an organisation being in your account, delete it.

## Deleting

Hover a row and click the bin. The confirmation names the organisation and how many projects it holds.

Deleting removes the organisation from Atlas along with its projects and their chats. **Your actual project files on disk are not touched.**

What's removed is Atlas's record of them: which projects belonged to the organisation, and the recent-chat history for those projects. It can't be undone.

You can't delete your last organisation.

For a cloud organisation, the server-side delete is admin-only. If you're not an admin it's refused with a message, and the organisation is still removed from this machine.

You leave it locally without deleting it for everyone else.
