Members are a server-side surface, so member management needs two things: a **cloud** [organisation](/docs/organisation/organisations), and a live sign-in. Signing out doesn't move you out of an organisation, so both are checked separately: the switcher will tell you which one is missing.

- Local organisation → *Turn on sync to manage members.*
- Cloud organisation, signed out → *Sign in to manage members.*

With both in place, **Invite & Manage members** in the switcher opens the roster.

## The roster

Two tabs, each with a count: **Members** for people who have joined, **Invitations** for invites that are outstanding or spent. A search box filters either by name or email.

Each member row shows their photo, name, email, role, and how long ago they joined. Your own row is marked **You**.

The roster is cached, so reopening it is instant and a refresh never blanks the table underneath you. The refresh button re-pulls it.

The Invitations tab is admin-only at the API level. If you're not an admin it simply comes back empty rather than showing an error.

## Inviting someone

Admins get an invite bar above the table. Type or paste one or more email addresses: they become chips, and a malformed one is called out before you can send.

Pick a role from the dropdown, then **Invite**.

Invites are sent one at a time rather than as a batch, so if one fails the rest still land.

<Callout type="warn">
  **The invite link is the invite.** Atlas doesn't deliver invitation emails yet, so nothing arrives in your teammate's inbox.

  On success the accept links are copied to your clipboard (*Invite link copied: send it to them*), and you have to pass them on yourself, over whatever channel you already use. Listing invitations later does **not** re-issue a link, so if you lose it, cancel the invitation and send a fresh one.
</Callout>

Once sent, the view switches to the Invitations tab so you can see what's pending. Admins can cancel any outstanding invitation from there.

## The four roles

Atlas has four roles, highest privilege first: **Admin**, **Product Owner**, **Developer**, and **Member**. Whoever creates an organisation is its admin.

A role is set at invite time and changed from the **⋯** menu on a member's row. Roles are enforced by the server, from claims carried in your access token.

The interface only hides buttons that would be refused anyway, so nothing is gated in the client alone.

<Callout type="info">
  **Inside Atlas, Admin is the only role that changes what you can do.** Product Owner, Developer, and Member are recorded on the account and shown against each person, but the desktop app grants all three the same access: they can open the organisation and read its roster, and that's it.

  The distinctions exist for what's built on top of them later. Treat them today as labels you're choosing in advance, not as permissions.
</Callout>

### What Admin unlocks

Only an admin can:

- Invite people, and choose the role they're invited at
- Change anyone's role
- Cancel a pending invitation
- Remove someone from the organisation
- See the Invitations tab at all
- Delete the organisation server-side

### What everyone else can do

Product Owner, Developer, and Member all get the same thing: open the organisation, see its projects, and view the member list. They have no invite bar, no row actions, and an empty Invitations tab.

### When a role change takes effect

A role change is applied immediately for you and shows in the roster right away. For the person whose role changed, it takes effect in **their next minted token**, so they may keep their old permissions briefly, until their client next refreshes.

Nothing is wrong if a promoted teammate can't invite anyone for a moment.

If a role change is refused by the server, the roster reverts to exactly what it was and tells you why.

### Unknown roles

If the server names a role this build of Atlas doesn't recognise (a fifth role added after your version shipped), the person's role renders as a dash rather than being guessed at.

They're still a member; only the label is missing. Updating Atlas resolves it.

The same applies to an organisation whose role you hold no claim for: it's listed without a role rather than hidden.

## Removing someone

An admin removes a member from the **⋯** menu on their row. It applies immediately and reverts with an explanation if the server refuses.

Removing yourself is **Leave organisation** in the same place, with one exception:

<Callout type="warn">
  **An admin can't leave.** Doing so could strip an organisation of its last admin, leaving nobody able to invite, change roles, or delete it.

  Give someone else the Admin role first, then leave. Removing *other* people (including other admins) stays allowed.
</Callout>
