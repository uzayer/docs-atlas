The Atlas beta programme is for people who want to test Atlas and help shape it. Think of it as being a contributor, plus a direct line to the team that builds it.

Atlas is a developer tool, so every tester is a developer. You don't need to write code for Atlas to take part: running it on your own work and telling us what happened is the most useful thing you can do.

## Who it's for

Anyone who wants to test and contribute can join. No one is turned away for team size, stack or platform.

The tester we learn the most from is on a team of roughly 3 to 30 people that already ships with coding agents. Atlas is built for teams working that way, so that is where its rough edges show first.

## How we work with testers

We run the programme inside Atlas itself. Testers join an organisation called **Atlas Contributors**, and that is where we talk: in its [team chat](/docs/team/chat) channels, on [calls](/docs/team/calls), and in [prompt drafts](/docs/team/prompt-drafts) when we write something together. There is no separate wiki or tracker for testers.

This is deliberate. Using Atlas to coordinate the people testing Atlas means we hit the same problems you do, at the same time.

The public [Discord](https://discord.gg/GmnFggaPfP) stays open to everyone. It is the front door for the wider community; the Atlas Contributors organisation is where beta work happens.

## What you get

- **AI credits for Atlas Agent**, through the Atlas credits programme, so you can run [Atlas Agent](/docs/agents/atlas-agent) on real work.
- **Early builds** to try before a release reaches everyone else.
- **A direct channel to the founding team** in the Atlas Contributors chat.
- **A say in the roadmap.** What testers run into shapes what we build next, and you can see where it lands on the [roadmap](/docs/community/roadmap).
- **Credit.** Contributors are named in the release notes and the [changelog](/docs/community/changelog).

## What we ask of you

- **Test the focus areas** we post for each release, on your own projects.
- **Report bugs with the session attached.** An agent session recorded in Atlas holds the prompts, tool calls and edits that led to the problem, which is usually enough for us to reproduce it.
- **Tell us what feels wrong**, even when nothing is technically broken.
- **Contribute code, if you want to.** It is optional.

[Testing Atlas](/docs/community/beta-testing) covers all of this in detail.

## How it works, end to end

1. You sign up at [credits.tryatlas.cc](https://credits.tryatlas.cc), the same site individuals and companies use to request AI credits for Atlas.
2. We add you to the Atlas Contributors organisation by hand.
3. You introduce yourself in its chat and pick up the current release's focus areas.
4. You use Atlas on your own work, and report what you find in the organisation's channels or on GitHub.
5. We fix things, ship them, and credit you when your report or code made it in.
